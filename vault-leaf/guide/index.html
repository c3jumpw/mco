How to use Vaultleaf

Vaultleaf keeps your passwords, keys, secret phrases and account details encrypted on your own device. Read accounts like your old notes, or as cards you can search and copy from. This guide shows every screen, with numbered callouts that match the notes beside each picture.

Who does what

Everyone

Anyone who opens Vaultleaf gets their own private vault in their own browser. They set their own password and passcode and manage their own accounts.

  • Set up and unlock
  • Find, copy and edit accounts
  • Use the Classic notes view
  • Back up and move devices

Admins

The person who runs the Vaultleaf website on Vercel and GitHub. Admins decide who can open the site and publish updates. They can't see or reset anyone's passwords.

  • Control access to the site
  • Publish updates and roll back
  • Help someone who's locked out
  • Explain how data is protected

Two kinds of "admin". Inside an account, the Admins section lists the people who manage that service, like the owner of a company AWS account. That's just information you store. It's separate from the admin who runs this website.

Set up your vault

Setup takes about a minute. Each device starts with its own setup, or with a backup from another device.

1

Add your name

  1. Name and email are optional. They appear on your backups.
  2. Have a backup? Restore it here instead.
2

Choose a master password

  1. Master password, at least 10 characters.
  2. Strength meter. It must reach Fair or better.
  3. Suggest a passphrase if you want one made for you.
3

Set a passcode

  1. Length: 4, 6 or 8 digits.
  2. Dots fill as you type.
  3. Keypad. A keyboard works too.
4

Save your recovery key

  1. Recovery key. Write it down or save it.
  2. Copy or download it.
  3. Confirm you saved it.
  4. Open your vault.
Keep your recovery key somewhere safe. It's the only way back in if you forget your password or passcode. Write it on paper, or keep the downloaded kit in a place other than this device. Nobody, including your admin, can reset your vault.

The last screen also offers four example accounts to explore. They're labeled Examples, and you can remove them in one tap from the banner above the list.

Unlock and lock

1. Password

Type your master password and press Continue.

2. Passcode

Tap your digits. Vaultleaf unlocks as soon as the last one is in.

Forgot?

Choose Forgot your password or passcode?, enter your recovery key, then pick new ones.

  • Wrong tries. If either part is wrong, Vaultleaf doesn't say which. After 5 wrong tries you wait 30 seconds, and the wait doubles each time up to 15 minutes.
  • Auto-lock. Vaultleaf locks after 5 minutes without activity, and when it has been in the background for over a minute. Change both in Settings.
  • Lock now. Use the lock button at the top of the list, or Lock at the bottom of the sidebar.

Find your way around

On a computer you see everything side by side. On a phone the open account slides over the list, and sections move to a bar at the bottom.

  1. Sections. Vault, Generator, Health, Activity, Settings and this Guide.
  2. Labels. Click one to see only those accounts. Sub-labels sit indented under their parent, and the counts include them.
  3. Search. Matches names, usernames, emails, websites, labels, notes and admins. Type #work to filter by label.
  4. View. Switch between Classic (notes) and Modern (cards).
  5. Lock. Locks right away. You need your password and passcode to get back in.
  6. Add. Start a new account. Shortcut: N.
  7. Quick copy. Copy the username or password without opening the account.
  8. The open account. Everything stored for it, with a copy button on every line.
  9. Sort. Recently edited, name, recently copied, date stored or leaf number.
  10. Status. Where your vault is saved, plus a Lock button.

On a phone

  1. Search everything except the secret values.
  2. Label chips. Tap one to filter. Swipe for more.
  3. View switch: Classic notes or Modern cards.
  4. Lock now.
  5. Quick copy username or password.
  6. Add an account.
  7. Bottom bar: Vault, Generator, Health, Activity, Settings.

Open an account

Every account is a page of its own. Tap any line to copy it, and Vaultleaf notes the time.

  1. Pin. Keeps this account at the top of the list.
  2. Edit. Change any detail. The old password is kept in History.
  3. More. Edit as note, duplicate, or delete.
  4. Open the website in a new tab.
  5. Strength rating. Extra pills warn when a password is reused or overdue.
  6. Show. Reveals the value. It hides itself again after 30 seconds.
  7. Copy. Copies the value and records the time. Tapping anywhere on the row does the same.
  8. 2FA code. A live code that changes every 30 seconds. The ring shows the time left.

Showing a hidden value

History and records

  1. Record. Leaf number, date stored, last edit, when the password was set, last copy and copy count.
  2. Changes. Every edit, with date and time.
  3. Old passwords and keys. Earlier values, which you can show and copy.
  4. Copies. Every time something was copied from this account.

Secret phrases

  1. Back to the list.
  2. Secret phrases appear as a numbered word grid.
  3. Hide or copy.

Classic and Modern views

Classic looks and works like the notes you keep passwords in now. Modern shows cards with copy buttons. Switch any time with the two icons above the list. Both show the same accounts.

  1. Folders. Your labels, shown like folders in a notes app.
  2. Date groups. Notes are grouped by when you last edited them. Pinned accounts sit on top.
  3. The open note is highlighted.
  4. Dates. When it was stored and last edited.
  5. Hidden values show as dots. Tap the dots to show them.
  6. Copy any line.

Writing an account as a note

  1. Type it like a note. First line is the name, then one Label: value per line.
  2. Insert password puts a new strong password where your cursor is.
  3. Edit as form. Switch editors any time without losing anything.
  4. Save. Vaultleaf turns the lines into fields.
  5. Cheat sheet of the labels Vaultleaf understands.

Add and edit accounts

Press Add, pick what kind of account it is, and fill in what you have. Every field except the name is optional.

  1. Website or app login. Username, email and password.
  2. Ready-made forms such as Crypto wallet add the right fields for you.
  3. Write it as a note, the Classic way.
  4. Import from Notes to add many at once.

The form editor

  1. Tool or service name, like Netflix or AWS.
  2. Account name, for when you have more than one account at the same service.
  3. Date stored. Set it to when you first saved the account, such as the date on your old note.
  4. Password, hidden while you type. Tap the eye to check it.
  5. Generate fills in a strong password.
  6. Strength meter with a guess-time estimate.
  1. Add a field: password, PIN, key or token, secret phrase, 2FA code, hidden text, text, link, email, phone, date or note.
  2. Security questions with hidden answers.
  3. Admins: the people who manage this account.
  4. Each admin has a name, email and role.
  1. Labels. Tap to add or remove.
  2. New label. Type Work/Cloud to make a sub-label.
  3. Reminder to change the password, from every 30 days to once a year.
  4. Save. The change is added to History.

More actions

  1. Edit as note: every detail as plain text.
  2. Duplicate: start a similar account.
  3. Delete. It stays in Recently deleted for 30 days.

Bring over your notes

You don't have to retype anything. Vaultleaf reads notes written the way most people write them, with lines like "user:" and "pass:".

  1. Paste one note or many.
  2. Separator. Tell Vaultleaf how your notes are split up.
  3. Label everything you import, such as Imported.
  4. Preview. Check the names and detail counts first.
  5. Import.
  1. In your notes app, open a note, select all, and copy.
  2. In Vaultleaf, go to Settings and choose Import from Notes, or use it from the Add menu.
  3. Paste. To bring many at once, put a line with --- between notes.
  4. Check the preview, then press Import. Each note becomes an account labeled Imported.

Coming from a browser or another password manager? Export a CSV file from it, then use Import a CSV file in Settings. Chrome, Safari, Bitwarden, 1Password and LastPass exports all work. Delete the CSV afterwards, because it isn't encrypted.

Labels and sub-labels

Labels work like folders, except an account can have several. In Classic view the sidebar calls them Folders.

  1. Name.
  2. Inside. Pick a parent to make this a sub-label.
  3. Color, used for the dot and the account tiles.
  • Create from the sidebar (New label), from Settings, or while editing an account by typing a name like Finance/Banks.
  • Sub-labels sit inside a parent. Picking the parent shows everything under it.
  • Search by label by typing # and part of its name, like #bank.
  • Deleting a label never deletes accounts. They just lose that label.

Make strong passwords

The generator uses your device's secure random number source. Passphrases are picked from a list of 7,776 words.

  1. Your new password. Numbers and symbols are colored so they are easier to read.
  2. Strength and how long it would take to guess.
  3. New password.
  4. Copy.
  5. Use for an account. Opens an account with this password filled in.
  6. Kind: random characters, or a passphrase of real words.
  7. Options: length and which characters to use.

Check password health

Health looks at every saved password and points out the ones worth changing.

  1. Score. The share of your passwords with no problems.
  2. Counts of weak, reused and overdue passwords.
  3. Tap any account to open it and fix it.

What counts as a problem

  • Weak: rated Weak or Very weak, like a dictionary word with a year.
  • Reused: the same password on more than one account.
  • Overdue: past its reminder, or not changed in over a year.
  • No 2FA code: a suggestion, not a problem. Turn on two-step sign-in where the site offers it.

Activity and copy records

Every copy and every change is recorded with the date and time, across all accounts.

  1. Filter: everything, copies only, or changes only.
  2. Each event shows what happened and to which account. Click to open it.
  3. Time. Hover for the full date.

Backups and new devices

Your vault is saved in this browser. A backup file is your copy for safekeeping and for moving to another device.

  1. Download the encrypted backup file.
  2. Copy it as text instead, then paste it into a note or an email to yourself.
  1. Choose the backup file.
  2. Or paste the backup text.
  3. Unlock with the password and passcode it was made with, or your recovery key.
  4. Enter them here.

Move to a new phone or computer

  1. On the old device: Settings, then Back up, then Download file (or Copy backup text).
  2. Get the file to the new device by AirDrop, email to yourself, or a cloud drive. It's encrypted, so that's safe.
  3. On the new device, open Vaultleaf and choose Restore from a backup file on the first screen.
  4. Pick the file and enter the password and passcode you had when you made it. Your vault opens.
Back up after big changes, and at least monthly. Settings shows the date of your last backup. A vault lives in one browser on one device, so a backup is what protects you if that browser's data is cleared or the device is lost.

Deleting and getting things back

  1. Recently deleted.
  2. Restore puts it back.
  3. Delete forever. This can't be undone.

Put it on your home screen

Installed this way, Vaultleaf opens full screen like an app, works offline, and keeps its data more reliably.

iPhone or iPad

  1. Open Vaultleaf in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button.
  3. Choose Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  4. From now on, open Vaultleaf from its icon.

Android

  1. Open Vaultleaf in Chrome.
  2. Tap the menu (three dots).
  3. Choose Add to Home screen or Install app.
  4. Open it from the new icon.
On iPhone, use the home-screen icon. Safari can clear a website's saved data if you haven't opened it for a week of browsing. Apps added to the home screen are kept. Either way, keep a recent backup.

After the first visit, Vaultleaf also opens without an internet connection.

How your data is protected

In plain terms: Vaultleaf scrambles your data with a key only you can make.

Your encrypted vault
Saved in this browser and in your backup files
  • Encrypted before it's saved. Every change is encrypted on your device before it's written to storage.
  • Your password and passcode never leave your device. They aren't stored anywhere, even in scrambled form.
  • No outside services. Vaultleaf loads nothing from other websites and sends nothing anywhere. The page blocks outside scripts on purpose.
  • Nobody can reset it. Not your admin, not Vercel, not GitHub. That's what keeps it private, and it's why the recovery key matters.
  • Backups are encrypted the same way. Only the CSV export is readable, so delete it after use.

Vaultleaf uses the standard encryption built into your browser. It hasn't been independently audited the way large commercial password managers have.

For admins

Admins look after the website itself. Everything inside a vault stays with its owner.

Control who can open the site

In Vercel, open the mco-vault-leaf project, then Settings, then Deployment Protection.

  • Vercel Authentication on (the current setting): only people logged in to your Vercel team can open the site. This is the most private option for a personal vault.
  • Off: anyone with the link can open it. Each person still gets an empty, private vault in their own browser, and can't see anyone else's.

Publish an update

Changes to the vault-leaf folder on the main branch of the c3jumpw/mco repository deploy automatically.

  • People get the new version the next time they open Vaultleaf online. Their saved data is untouched.
  • If an update misbehaves, use Instant Rollback on the project's Vercel page.

Keep the web address

Each vault belongs to one web address in one browser. If you move Vaultleaf to a custom domain, everyone needs to back up on the old address first and restore on the new one.

Help someone who's locked out

  • Forgot password or passcode: they use their recovery key on the unlock screen.
  • Lost the key too, but remember the password and passcode: they make a new key in Settings.
  • New or lost device: they restore their latest backup file.
  • Without any of these, the vault can't be opened. You have no way to reset it.

Keep it private

  • Leave Vercel Web Analytics and Speed Insights off. The site blocks outside scripts on purpose.
  • Security headers live in vault-leaf/vercel.json. Keep them when you edit.
  • The site asks search engines not to list it.

Troubleshooting

I forgot my master password or passcode.
On the unlock screen choose Forgot your password or passcode?, enter your recovery key, and pick new ones.
I lost my recovery key.
If you can still unlock, go to Settings and choose New recovery key, then save it. If you can't unlock and don't have the key, the vault can't be opened by anyone. Restore a backup if you have one, using the password and passcode from when you made it.
It says "Too many tries".
After 5 wrong tries you must wait before trying again. The wait starts at 30 seconds and doubles up to 15 minutes. Check Caps Lock, then try again when the timer ends.
My accounts are gone.
Check you're on the same device, browser and web address you set up on. Each is a separate vault. If the browser's data was cleared, restore your latest backup.
Copy doesn't work.
Some browsers block copying in certain cases. Tap the eye to show the value and copy it by hand. On iPhone, use Safari or the home-screen icon.
It locked while I was using it.
Auto-lock starts after 5 minutes without taps or typing. Change it in Settings, under Lock automatically.
Can I use it without internet?
Yes, after you've opened it once online. Everything you do works offline.
Is it safe to email myself a backup?
Yes. The backup is encrypted and opens only with your password and passcode or recovery key. The CSV export is different: it isn't encrypted.
