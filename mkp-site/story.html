The long version

Deconstructing things since I could pick up a device.

My family is from Sierra Leone, West Africa, and I have spent most of my life in Atlanta. I started as a web developer and never lost the habit of taking things apart to see how they work. First code, then whole businesses.

Building websites meant learning design, so I taught myself graphic design to go with it. Copywriting and systems came naturally. What I ended up with is an unusual mix: the creative designer, the technician and the marketer in one person. It meant I could take a client from a new look all the way through to what that look actually earned them.

Freelancing put me in front of nearly every kind of business. Hospitality, finance, ecommerce, retail, technical services, government contracting, and white-label work for other marketers and PR firms. Going door to door, working events and doing outreach taught me the part nobody teaches a developer, which is how to sell.

In Atlanta I became known as the designer behind a lot of prominent restaurants and clubs, while quietly handling branding, design and marketing for small businesses that needed all three from one person.

It was always the systems business, even when it looked like web design. Owners wanted a way to do business without having to be in the room, or to share information without being the one sharing it. That is what a website is. Over the years the same instinct grew into building the operational systems underneath.

The rest was learned the expensive way. Hiring and firing, rejections, failed projects, systems that did not hold. Smaller corporate jobs taken deliberately to watch how they ran from the inside, and more failures there. Hundreds of seminars, business books, lectures and mentors on top of it. What came out is a set of practices I run on my own companies before I ever hand them to a client.

These days I split my time between serving clients and planting seeds for next year: building ventures, shipping projects, and supporting communities overseas.

True success is sitting in the shade of a tree someone planted years before, and making sure you are planting those trees for other people too.
