Browser app

Passwords that never leave the device.

Vault Leaf keeps passwords, keys, recovery phrases and account details encrypted on the machine you are using. There is no sync service in the middle, no company holding a master key, and nobody, including whoever runs the site, who can read or reset your vault.

A password manager you can see the whole of.

Every mainstream password manager asks you to trust a company you cannot inspect with the one file that opens everything else. Usually that trust is well placed. The problem is that you have no way of checking, and the breach notice always arrives after the fact.

Vault Leaf moves the trust somewhere you can see. Your vault is encrypted in your own browser with a master password and a passcode, and it stays there. Backups are files you hold. Moving to a new device means carrying one of those files across yourself.

The tradeoff is honest and worth stating plainly: because nobody holds a key, nobody can give you a way back in. A recovery key is generated at setup, and if you lose it along with your password, the vault is gone. That is the cost of the guarantee, not a flaw in it.

How it works

Set up in a minute

Pick a master password, set a passcode, and save the recovery key that gets generated. Four example accounts come loaded so there is something to explore, removable in one tap.

Unlock and work

Password, then passcode. Add accounts, copy fields, organize with labels, and generate new passwords in place.

Back up on your terms

An encrypted backup file you keep. Restore it on another device to carry the vault across, with nothing in between.

What is in it

Six sections, and no accounts to administer.

Two part unlock

A master password of at least ten characters with a strength meter, then a four, six or eight digit passcode. A wrong try does not say which part was wrong.

Lockout that escalates

After five wrong attempts the wait is thirty seconds, and it doubles each time up to fifteen minutes. Auto-lock runs after five minutes idle, or a minute in the background.

Labels and sub-labels

Nested labels with counts that include their children, so a group can be read at the level you care about.

Search across everything

Names, usernames, emails, websites, labels, notes and the admins listed on an account, all matched at once.

Classic notes view

A second way to read the same vault, laid out like the plain notes file many people kept before this, for anyone who thinks in paragraphs rather than cards.

Generator

New passwords and passphrases made in place, at the moment an account needs one.

Health

A standing read on the vault: what is weak, what is reused, what has sat unchanged too long.

Activity

A log of what happened in the vault and when, kept on the device with everything else.

Practical details

  • RunsIn the browser. Each person who opens it gets their own private vault in their own browser.
  • EncryptionEverything is encrypted on the device, under your master password and passcode.
  • RecoveryA recovery key issued at setup is the only way back in. Nobody else can reset a vault, by design.
  • BackupsEncrypted backup files you hold and move yourself, for restoring or changing device.
  • AdminWhoever runs the site decides who can open it and publishes updates. They cannot see or reset anyone's vault.
