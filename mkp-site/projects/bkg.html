Browser app

One logo in. A whole brand kit out.

BKG takes a single logo and produces everything a brand actually needs in use: the horizontal and vertical lockups, the white version, the icons at every size, the palette and the file formats. It runs entirely in the browser and hands the lot back as a zip.

The files nobody has when they need one.

A business has a logo. What it does not have is the white version for a dark background, the square icon for a social profile, the favicon, the transparent PNG, or the hex codes written down anywhere findable. Those get made one at a time, usually badly, usually in the ten minutes before somebody needs one.

BKG turns that scramble into a five step run. Bring in the logo, separate the mark from the wordmark, save the variations, generate the icons, pull the colors, download the whole kit as a zip.

Everything happens on the machine you are sitting at. Images go into a Library, every tool reads from the Library and writes results back into it for the next tool, and nothing is uploaded anywhere. There are no accounts and no roles. Your files stay in your own browser.

A look inside

Five steps, one logo, a folder of finished files.

Step one

A checklist, not a canvas

The Brand Kit page is a checklist with five items on it: choose your logo, save the logo variations, make the icons, confirm the colors, download the kit. Each one hands off to the tool that does it and brings you back when it is done.

It is deliberately not a blank canvas. You are not designing anything here, you are producing the files a designer already decided on.

Step two

Separate the mark from the wordmark

Logo Creator is a layer editor built for one job. It splits a logo into its parts, either automatically or with a precise crop you draw yourself, so the icon can live without the text beside it.

Quick arrange snaps the mark and the text into a horizontal or vertical lockup as a starting point, and you drag from there. Save each one as a named variation and it goes into the kit.

Step three

Icons at every size something asks for

Asset Creator frames the mark inside the squares and rounded squares that profiles, favicons and app icons expect, with padding and background handled rather than guessed at.

One pass produces the whole set, so the next time a platform asks for a 512 pixel square there is already one in the folder.

Step four

The colors, written down at last

Palette Extractor reads the colors straight out of the artwork and lists them as HEX, RGB and HSL, ready to copy into a stylesheet, a deck or a brief.

They also become the brand palette in the sidebar, one click from a copied hex code on every page of the app.

Alongside

Five quick tools for the awkward jobs

Background Remover clears a solid or gradient background and returns a transparent PNG, with tolerance and edge softness to control how hard it cuts.

Beside it sit Recolor for one colour variants and stamps, Text Extractor for pulling the words out of an image, Palette Extractor, and Resize and Convert. Each does one job, and each can send its result back to the Library for the next tool.
