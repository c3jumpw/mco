Web app

A new hire's first month, run as a track.

RAMP is company training and HR in one place. Somebody is invited, works through the documents and modules their role needs, has their work reviewed by a mentor, and graduates to full access when they are actually ready for it.

Request a demo All projects

Onboarding is usually a folder and somebody's memory.

A welcome email with attachments. A handbook nobody opens twice. Policies signed on paper and filed where no one can find them. Whoever happens to be free walks the new person around, and what they are told depends on who was free that week.

It holds together at two hires a year. At ten it stops, usually at the moment somebody asks who confirmed that the new crew read the safety policy, and the honest answer is that nobody knows.

RAMP makes the first month a track. The documents are forms that stamp themselves. The training is modules with a pass mark and a mentor's sign-off. Access is restricted until the track is finished. What comes out the other side is a person who is ready, and a record that says so.

A look inside

From the invitation to the day they graduate.

Day zero

An invite, and a login that matches it

HR adds the person in People, picks their access level, role and mentor, and sends them a link. They choose New here and sign up with the address the invite went to, which is how RAMP ties the login to the record that is already waiting for them.

A different address will not match, which is the point. Nobody signs themselves into your company's training by guessing a URL.

First hour

A first hour that is already laid out

Six steps: a welcome message, their profile, a practice tour of the app, the onboarding documents, meeting their mentor, and a finish screen. They can stop and come back; it picks up where they left it.

The documents step is the one that usually eats a week of back and forth. Here it is a list with a counter on it, and each item is a form, an upload, an e-signature or a checklist.

Learning the tool

Five minutes of practice before anything counts

A sandbox copy of RAMP with nine short missions. The dark card says what to do, an amber outline glows around the spot to tap, and a green note confirms it. Nothing done in there is saved against anything.

It solves the problem every internal tool has, which is that the first day is spent learning the tool instead of the job. It also doubles as the first training module, so finishing the tour ticks a real box.

The work

Assigned training, and a library behind it

Modules are built from slides, readings, videos, quizzes, assignments, forms and the practice sandbox. They arrive on three tracks: what everyone does, what the role does on top, and continued training that keeps coming after onboarding.

Every module runs the same path. Learn, then a quiz with a pass mark, then a written assignment, then a mentor's review, then complete. Progress saves as they go, and the status on each card says exactly where it is stuck.

Paperwork

Forms that are records, not PDFs in an inbox

Bank details, tax forms, ID uploads, policy acknowledgements. Fields with a lock are encrypted and only the owner and HR admins can reveal them, and every reveal is logged. Files up to 25 MB attach to the answer they belong to.

Signing is typing a full legal name. On submit, RAMP stamps the time, the IP address and the device, so what you keep is a record with provenance rather than a signed PDF somebody emailed from a phone.

Everyday

After onboarding, it is just where work lives

Tasks move left to right across To do, In progress, In review and Done. Work that needs a second pair of eyes goes to review rather than quietly shipping.

Alongside it: a private notebook they can share a page of with their mentor, company and department chat, a searchable shelf of policies and SOPs, announcements that ask to be confirmed read, and a personal encrypted vault for work logins that not even an admin can open.

Behind the scenes

The side HR and the owners see.

Setup

A checklist that sets the thing up

Organization and brand, roles and departments, documents, training modules, assign training, welcome flow, invite people, watch metrics. Eight steps in order, tracked for you, so standing it up is not a project.

Brand colour runs through buttons, highlights and the welcome screens, including the sign-in page, so it reads as the company's own tool rather than something bought.

Access

Seven access levels, enforced in the database

Owner, admin, lead, mentor, employee, trainee and guest. A new hire sits at trainee until an admin graduates them, at which point they move to the level their job role carries.

The rules are enforced at the row level in the database, not just hidden in the interface, which is the difference between a permission and a suggestion. Guest access is time limited by default.

Content

Modules built from blocks, assigned by role

Start from a template, then add blocks: slides, reading, video, quiz, assignment, form or e-sign, or the practice sandbox. Fold blocks away while you work on another, reorder them, write the quiz and tick the right answer.

Then decide who gets what. Switch a module on for everyone, or add it to a role's track in the order it should be done. Due dates run from each person's start date.

Evidence

An audit log you can hand to someone

Every sign-in, signature, submission, reveal and file open, with who, when, the IP address and the device. Searchable by word or by person, exportable as a spreadsheet, with reveals and file opens highlighted.

Feature switches turn whole tools on or off without deleting anything, and only owners can change them. RAMP flags sign-ins from new IP addresses and devices to the person they belong to.

In practice

Three moments it earns its keep

Hiring in groups

The tenth hire gets the same first week as the first

Onboarding usually lives in one person's memory and degrades every time they are busy. A track that is written down once runs the same way in a hiring spike as it does in a quiet month.

Regulated or safety-sensitive work

Proof, not an assurance

Signed acknowledgements with a time, an IP address and a device against them, quiz scores against a pass mark, and an exportable log of who opened what. The evidence is a by-product of people doing the work.

A mentor carrying three new people

Review instead of chase

Work arrives in one queue when it is ready. Approve it, or return it with feedback, which RAMP requires you to write. Nobody has to remember who is waiting on whom.

How it works

Set it up once

Work the admin checklist: brand, roles and departments, the documents people sign, the modules they take, who gets which, and the welcome flow they land in.

They run the track

Invite, login, welcome flow, practice sandbox, then the modules their role needs. Documents get filled, signed and stamped on the way through.

Someone signs it off

A mentor approves the work or returns it with feedback. When the track is done, an admin graduates them from trainee to the access level their job actually carries.
