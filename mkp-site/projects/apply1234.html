Web app

The application is the first screening test.

apply1234 turns rough notes on a role into a job posting with the application link hidden inside it. Candidates who read closely enough to find the link answer your questions on video, and you review, score and interview them in one place.

Request a demo All projects

Everybody applies to everything now.

One click sends a resume, so a decent posting draws hundreds of them and almost none of the senders have read it. All the screening work lands on you, and the first real signal about a person arrives at interview, weeks in.

apply1234 moves that signal to the front. The posting carries the application link broken into pieces and scattered through the text. Finding the page means reading the whole thing and putting it back together, which is a small amount of work that only somebody genuinely interested will do.

What reaches you is a short stack of people who read the role, answered your questions on video, and left a record of how they worked while doing it.

A look inside

Notes to posting, posting to shortlist.

Step one

Rough notes in, a posting out

Paste the bullet points you already have: duties, must-haves, schedule, pay, what good looks like. Leave the title blank and it suggests one.

Pick how many video questions to ask, between three and six, and how long each answer should run. The AI prompt is then built for you, carrying your notes and the exact format the reply has to come back in, so the posting, the questions and the scoring rubric all arrive together.

The idea

A link nobody can guess, broken into pieces

Every job gets a random address that is checked for collisions as you type, and the site never lists its jobs anywhere. The only way in is the link.

Then the link is split, and the pieces are scattered through the posting at points you choose. Finding the page means reading the whole thing and putting it back together. That one step does the first cut before you have read a single résumé.

Review

Video answers and the rubric on one screen

Each question is answered on video, so you hear how somebody thinks rather than how well they write with help. The answers sit beside the rubric that came with the posting, and you score, tag and make notes without leaving the page.

Flags run along the top: whether every link is a real recording, whether the answers came in at the length you asked for, and whether the hidden code word made it into the video titles.

Dashboard

What happened, and what needs you

New applicants against the period before, page visits, starts, unread messages, who is waiting on review, the shortlist and upcoming interviews. The numbers in orange are the ones that need a person.

The funnel underneath shows how many got from the link to a finished application, which is the number that tells you whether the posting is doing its job. Your own test visits are never counted.

Second opinion

Hand the whole candidate to a model

Export builds a single document holding the role, the questions, the rubric, the candidate's answers, their signals, your scores and your notes, with instructions for a reviewer at the top.

Turn on hiding names and emails and it becomes a blind review. The same thing works across everyone who applied, when you want them compared rather than read one at a time.

In practice

Three moments it earns its keep

A role that draws hundreds

The first cut happens before you read

One click sends a résumé, so a good posting now draws hundreds from people who never read it. The hidden link filters on effort rather than on keywords, and what lands in your inbox is a short stack.

Hiring for attention

Proof somebody follows instructions

A code word that has to appear in every video title. Link checks that fail when the thing pasted is not a recording. Wrong attempts logged on the way in. For a role where detail is the job, that is the evidence.

A panel that cannot meet

How it works

Set the job up

Notes in, AI draft out, hidden link chosen and split, questions and rubric confirmed, then post it to the boards you use with one link per board.

They apply

A candidate reads the posting, assembles the link, records an answer per question and submits, with an optional résumé. Everything they do on the way is timed and logged.

You decide

Watch, score against the rubric, read the behavior panel as context, shortlist, run the guided interview, then invite or decline.

What is in it

Built around one idea: make the application itself tell you something.

A link split through the posting

A random address no one can guess, broken into pieces placed at points you choose. Reading the posting properly is the only way in.

A hidden code word

The posting asks for a word in every video title, and the app checks for it. A quiet test of whether somebody read to the end.

Video answers

Three to six questions, one recording each, at a length you set. You hear how a person reasons rather than how well they prompt.

Behavior signals

Wrong attempts and what they were, time from arriving to submitting, time on each step, how often they switched tabs, failed link checks, which board they came from, device and time zone.

A rubric that arrives with the posting

The same draft that writes the job writes what a good answer looks like, so scoring is against something written before anyone applied.

Export for AI review

One document per candidate, or for the whole field, with an option to hide names and emails for a blind read.

Overview dashboard

Applicants per day, the funnel from link to finished application, and a needs-attention list. Your own visits are excluded.

Inbox and activity log

Messages candidates send from the Help button in one place, and a live log of visits, wrong attempts and submissions.

Practical details

  • RunsIn the browser, as a private admin area. Candidates need no account and install nothing.
  • QuestionsThree to six per job, answered on video, at a length you choose per role.
  • SignalsWrong attempts, time to find the page, time per step, active time, tab switches, failed link checks, referrer, device and time zone.
  • TeamEveryone on the hiring team shares the same access. Alerts are set per person.
  • TrackingTied to one browser, so somebody who switches device starts fresh and their numbers cover only the device they submitted from.
  • Signals are contextThey sit next to the answers and never decide anything on their own.
