The story

Deconstructing things since I could pick up a device.

My family is from Sierra Leone, West Africa, and I have spent most of my life in Atlanta. I started as a web developer and never lost the habit of taking things apart to see how they work. First code, then whole businesses.

Building websites meant learning design, so I taught myself graphic design to go with it. Copywriting and systems came naturally. What I ended up with is an unusual mix: the creative designer, the technician and the marketer in one person. It meant I could take a client from a new look all the way through to what that look actually earned them.

Freelancing put me in front of nearly every kind of business. Hospitality, finance, ecommerce, retail, technical services, government contracting, and white-label work for other marketers and PR firms. Going door to door, working events and doing outreach taught me the part nobody teaches a developer, which is how to sell.

In Atlanta I became known as the designer behind a lot of prominent restaurants and clubs, while quietly handling branding, design and marketing for small businesses that needed all three from one person.

It was always the systems business, even when it looked like web design. Owners wanted a way to do business without having to be in the room, or to share information without being the one sharing it. That is what a website is. Over the years the same instinct grew into building the operational systems underneath.

The rest was learned the expensive way. Hiring and firing, rejections, failed projects, systems that did not hold. Smaller corporate jobs taken deliberately to watch how they ran from the inside, and more failures there. Hundreds of seminars, business books, lectures and mentors on top of it. What came out is a set of practices I run on my own companies before I ever hand them to a client.

True success is sitting in the shade of a tree someone planted years before, and making sure you are planting those trees for other people too.

The shelf

What I actually studied.

The continuing education happened outside a classroom. These are the books that left a mark, and that I still hand to clients when the problem they are describing has already been written about properly.

  • The Ultimate Sales Machine Chet Holmes Pigheaded discipline on twelve things, rather than scattered effort across a hundred.
  • The E-Myth Revisited Michael Gerber Names the trap exactly: working in the business instead of on it. Build it so it runs without you.
  • $100M Offers Alex Hormozi How to build an offer good enough that price stops being the conversation.
  • Execution Larry Bossidy & Ram Charan Strategy fails on delivery, not on ideas. The discipline of actually getting things done.
  • Rich Dad Poor Dad Robert Kiyosaki Assets against liabilities, and the gap between earning money and owning something.
  • Think and Grow Rich Napoleon Hill Definiteness of purpose and the mastermind. Old, and still the root of most of what followed.
  • Secrets of Closing the Sale Zig Ziglar Selling as service rather than persuasion, with the mechanics underneath it.
  • Hooked Nir Eyal The trigger, action, reward and investment loop behind products people come back to unprompted.
  • Millionaire Success Habits Dean Graziosi Small changes to the daily inputs, compounding into different outputs.
  • How to Win Friends and Influence People Dale Carnegie Still the clearest book written on paying genuine attention to other people.
  • The 10X Rule Grant Cardone Most goals fail from under-commitment rather than bad luck. Set bigger, act accordingly.
  • The Alchemist Paulo Coelho A reminder that the work and the journey turn out to be the same thing.
  • Blue Ocean Strategy Kim & Mauborgne Compete less by building somewhere the competition is not already standing.
  • Atomic Habits James Clear Systems over goals, which is the same argument I end up making to business owners.
  • Million Dollar Weekend Noah Kagan Validate fast and ask for money early, instead of polishing before anyone has said yes.
  • Buy Back Your Time Dan Martell Buy back the draining hours in the right order, rather than hiring at random.
  • Relentless Tim Grover What operating at the top actually costs, written without softening any of it.
  • Expert Secrets Russell Brunson Turning expertise into something people want to belong to, not just read.
  • DotCom Secrets Russell Brunson The funnel mechanics sitting underneath most of modern online selling.
  • The Power of Now Eckhart Tolle Attention as the one thing you genuinely control.
  • Sell Like Crazy Sabri Suby Direct response treated as a system to run, rather than a mood to be in.
  • Influence Robert Cialdini The six reasons people say yes, and how to notice them being used on you.
  • The Compound Effect Darren Hardy Small consistent action, run long enough, beating short bursts of intensity.
